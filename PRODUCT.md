# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

AgriPulse serves two co-primary audiences, and the design must treat them as equals.

**LGU agricultural officers** in the BLISTT area of the Cordillera, Philippines are the authenticated power users. Their context is a desk and a dashboard: they monitor community planting patterns, crop-saturation risk, and incoming submissions, then act — steering seed distribution and advising farmers before a market crash. Their job is turning a district's scattered field data into a decision.

**Smallholder farmers** are the field users, usually on a low-end phone, often on a slow rural connection, across Tagalog, Ilocano, and English. Their job is to report what they planted in about thirty seconds, check their status and standing, and get back a reason to keep doing it — price advice and rewards. The guiding benchmark the product sets for itself is "if farmers can use Facebook, they can use this."

## Product Purpose

AgriPulse exists to move farming from reactive to predictive. Today farmers plant blind: when everyone plants cabbage prices collapse, when few plant carrots prices spike, produce rots unsold, and agricultural offices distribute seed without knowing what is in the ground. AgriPulse turns simple, community-powered crop submissions into real-time agricultural intelligence — so the boom-and-bust cycle of oversupply crashes and shortage spikes can be seen coming and interrupted.

Success is defined the way the product states it publicly: measurably steadier farmer income (a 25–40% income-stabilization target), less waste (around 30% post-harvest reduction), and materially more efficient LGU planning (~60%). In plain terms, success is farmers earning steadier and officers intervening before the crash instead of reacting after it.

## Positioning

AgriPulse turns individual farmer crop submissions into real-time municipal market intelligence, so oversupply and shortage can be predicted and prevented rather than survived. Community-powered data in, predictive intervention out — that is the single claim every screen reinforces.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, empowering, trustworthy. The voice is warm and plain-spoken, confident without hype — it takes the anxiety of "planting blind" and hands the user control. It reads as premium and credible without becoming institutional; approachable without becoming toy-like. The emotional target is reassurance at the moments that touch livelihood: hope and steadiness where a farmer would otherwise feel exposed.

## Anti-references

AgriPulse should not look like any of these, and each is a real failure mode to steer away from:

- **A sterile government portal** — gray, dense, bureaucratic, intimidating form-first design.
- **A cold corporate agritech SaaS** — navy-and-gray chart-soup with no human context or warmth.
- **A childish, cartoonish app** — over-illustrated and playful to the point of undermining the income stakes.
- **A flashy crypto/fintech product** — neon gradients and hype energy, exactly the wrong trust signal for farmers and LGU staff.

## Design Principles

Predictive over reactive — surfaces should lead with what is coming (saturation risk, projected harvest, the intervention window), not only what already happened.

Approachable authority — earn trust as a data system without feeling bureaucratic or corporate; the Facebook-simple bar for farmers and the report-grade credibility for officers are the same principle from two ends.

Two audiences, one system — serve the mobile, fast, multilingual farmer path and the dense, analytical officer path as equals; never compromise one to decorate the other.

Income is the stakes — make consequences legible and reassure at livelihood-touching moments; a wrong planting decision costs a cycle of income, and the design should carry that weight honestly.

Preserve the earthy identity — the established deep-green, harvest-lime, and grey-turquoise (#5d9e87) theme is the brand. Extend and refine it; do not restyle it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

A multilingual farmer interface (Tagalog / Ilocano / English) and mobile-first submission on low-end phones are core product realities, not add-ons. Color-contrast ratios are already tuned and documented in the token layer, and reduced-motion is handled globally. No additional formal accessibility requirements were specified beyond these; revisit and set an explicit WCAG target if a compliance need arises.
